Antifa isn't a workers' movement, and it isn't a resistance. It's a bourgeois hobby: comfortable, mostly middle-class activists, writing in English across every Western country, staging a revolution the state pays for. Take away the funding and the flattering media coverage and very little would be left.

## The language of the empire

Look at how it talks. The slogans, the graffiti, and the group chats are in English, whether the country is Germany, France, Sweden, or Austria. It isn't a movement of any people in particular. It's an international, online subculture speaking the language of the American empire, and much of its rhetoric is aimed at its own: the native, the white, the male.

That combination says a lot about who joins. These aren't the oppressed rising up. They're mostly the children of the comfortable, often self-loathing, turning their resentment of their own background into politics. It's the same type every time: a little bourgeois antifa, rebelling against the society that feeds him.

## Activism as religion

People underestimate how much leftist activism is a social and religious act rather than a political one. You go to have a good time, to be among your own, to signal what a good person you are, to feel important and righteous. Results are beside the point. Actually fixing anything would be counterproductive, because it would end the ritual.

That's why the spectacle repeats so reliably. In Germany it looks like a pointless autoimmune disease. Every opposition rally draws a larger and more heavily ritualised counter-protest, and a larger police operation to keep it from turning violent. A few officers get injured, a few journalists get roughed up, the event goes ahead untouched, and the whole thing achieved nothing until next time. As politics it fails. As liturgy it works perfectly.

## The funding funnel

None of this is hidden. Money for the activist scene is at most half-heartedly laundered through one or two layers of NGOs. Senior politicians in governing parties are open about their sympathy for antifa, and the parties further left are more open still.

The model is the same across the West. Taxes go to NGOs, and NGOs fund activists. Give your organisation a name like "The Institute for Democracy and Tolerance" and the grants never stop. In Austria, so-called "civil society" is mostly financed by taxpayers or compulsory chamber contributions. Its extremists are less violent than Germany's, and some of the violence they do have is imported from across the border. Substitute any Western country for Germany and the picture fits.

So the "grassroots" aren't grassroots. A state-funded network protests against the state's opponents and calls it civil society.

## Defund the apparatus

That points to the first priority for any serious right-wing party, and it should be number one on the agenda: defund the NGOs, the state broadcasters, and the subsidised "cultural" groups. Not argue with them, not out-protest them, not try to win them over. Cut off the money.

Every other reform fails while this machine survives, because it is the machine that produces the activists, the narratives, and the pressure against reform. Without public money, the whole apparatus has to survive on what its own believers will pay for, and that is a fraction of what it has now.

## Where this leads

Movements that live on state money aren't rebellions. They're departments. The day the funding stops, the revolution will find it has other plans for the weekend.

Stop paying for your own opposition.
