The EU has its priorities backwards. It standardises what doesn't matter and leaves undone what does. Europe needs a common army and a single market that actually works. What it gets instead is matching licence plates and faceless banknotes.

## Uniformity for its own sake

Look at the small things. Every American state has its own licence plates, with its own colours, slogans, and character. Across the EU the plates all look more or less the same. Euro banknotes are the same story: identical bridges and windows that belong to no real place, printed for twenty countries that each have centuries of their own figures and symbols to put on them.

None of this serves any purpose. A Danish or Polish note wouldn't be less of a euro for carrying a Danish or Polish face. The coins already prove it, since each country stamps its own side and the money spends the same everywhere. Uniformity here isn't a need. It's the Commission standardising because it can, to show that Europe is "unifying" and to flex its power.

## What unity is actually for

There are things Europe really should do together. A common European army. A common legal framework for investment, so capital can move across the continent as easily as it moves across America. Fewer internal barriers to trade, work, and building things. These are the projects that would turn Europe into a power that can defend itself and compete with the US and China.

They are also the projects that keep getting stuck. Brussels finds it far easier to harmonise the look of a banknote than to build a military or tear down the barriers that keep capital and companies small.

## Sameness makes Europe scarier

This tendency does real damage. Every needless act of uniformity tells Europeans that joining means disappearing. That is exactly why countries like Denmark, Czechia, and Poland still keep their own currencies. Let each eurozone country print notes with its own figures and symbols and a good part of the opposition would melt away. People will share a currency far more readily than they will give up a face.

The principle is simple. Encourage diversity wherever possible, let nations express their individuality, and unify only where it serves Europe's common interests. An EU built that way would seem far less threatening, and far more people would want to be part of it.

## Dissolution or power

The reason the EU gets this backwards is that much of the integrationist project was never about making Europe a global force. In its liberal form it is about getting rid of European national identities. For the people who hold that view, Europe is a kind of Buddhist dissolution ritual: the goal is for the self to disappear, and every distinct nation is an attachment to be let go of.

That vision produces exactly what we see: endless pressure on symbols and identity, and paralysis on hard power. A Europe that wanted to matter would do the reverse. It would let its nations stay themselves and combine their strength where strength counts.

Unify the army, not the licence plates.
