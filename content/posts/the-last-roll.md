Modern dating has men fighting each other over women the way people fought over the last roll of single-ply in 2020. Friends become rivals, and a friendship that used to be a refuge becomes one more front in the competition.

## The family as the test

Chesterton put the stakes plainly:

> An honest man falls in love with an honest woman; he wishes, therefore, to marry her, to be the father of her children, to secure her and himself. All systems of government should be tested by whether he can do this. If any system—feudal, servile, or barbaric—does, in fact, give him so large a cabbage-field that he can do it, there is the essence of liberty and justice. If any system—Republican, mercantile, or Eugenist—does, in fact, give him so small a salary that he can't do it, there is the essence of eternal tyranny and shame.

By that test, a society where ordinary men can't find a wife and afford a family is failing, however free it calls itself.

## A distorted picture

The internet has warped how each sex sees the other, as it has warped nearly everything. Freer information was supposed to cure ignorance. Instead it gave everyone an endless feed of the worst and most extreme examples of the other side.

Part of the answer is old-fashioned. Raise boys on real food, hard physical work, and daily sport rather than screens, and they grow into men who don't need to measure themselves against their friends. The only competition worth winning is with the man you were yesterday.

I'm not optimistic that individuals can fix the system itself. Those who run things now have more surveillance, data, and leverage over human behaviour than any ruling class in history. Humans have always been good at understanding animals, and we've now added ourselves to the list. Control has never been more certain, and revolt has never been harder. What's left is the part each man can still do for himself: keep his friends, and build a family.
