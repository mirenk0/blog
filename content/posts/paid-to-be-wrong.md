Academics don't have to produce anything. A professor can be wrong about everything and keep the title, the prestige, and the salary. A job like that attracts ideologues and midwits, and over time it fills up with them. Some ideas are so insane that only academics could believe them.

## Memory isn't thought

The academic mistakes memorising for thinking. Years of absorbing what others have said produce someone who knows a lot and has rarely thought for themselves. That's why academia almost never innovates. Radically new theories seldom come out of a consensus, because a consensus has an internal whip that keeps everyone in line. Inside it, contradicting the worldview becomes almost impossible to even imagine, and anyone outside who suggests it might be wrong gets dismissed as an uneducated fool.

## Universities were guilds

It wasn't always like this. The old universities were built like guilds. Masters trained apprentices, and the master's thesis was a masterpiece in the craft sense: the work that proved you were skilled enough to join.

Some places still work this way. Their universities and polytechnics stay close to industry, and students move almost seamlessly from study to work. Engineering and the hard sciences are held in high regard, and the social sciences are seen as the fallback for people who can't do maths. Compare the American university, which often runs less like a guild than a marketing firm and a centre for political propaganda. The difference isn't an accident. A guild is answerable to the craft. A propaganda centre is answerable to nobody.

## Producers and scribes

The resentment runs both ways, and it's ancient. An Egyptian scribe's text from four thousand years ago spends page after page mocking the smith, the potter, the farmer, and the fisherman, so the student will see that the scribe's life is better than all of theirs. It has always been the bureaucrat against the producer.

The pattern holds through history. Medieval students were often younger sons of the nobility, too proud to work and bitter about not inheriting the family estate. Arrogance and envy are a dangerous mix. When the uprisings of the industrial age came, the people who took them over weren't the workers. They were educated men, good at rhetoric, who despised the people they claimed to lead. In a word, Marxists. And it wasn't only communism. Academics lined up behind fascism and other tyrannies too, with prominent professors in Germany among the regime's most eager supporters. That part is rarely taught.

A professor once told a room of students that they were "the top of society". Twenty-year-old broke English majors, the top of society. The belief never changes, only who holds it.

## What earns respect

Nobody hates the university that produces inventors. When academics create technology that improves everyday life, they are rightly honoured. The contempt is for the other kind: social scientists who want to run everyone's daily life in order to test their theories. Students follow them, so absorbed in activism that you have to wonder when they find time to study.

## Keep them out of power

The fix is simple. Academics are lifelong children who have never had to make anything work. They can teach, research, and argue all they like, but they should have no say over government or social policy. Return the university to the guild model, answerable to the craft and to the people who use what it produces, and judge every scholar the way a tradesman is judged.

If your work doesn't have to work, you shouldn't get to run anyone's life.
