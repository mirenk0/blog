AI and robotics will do everything. Jobs will stop being necessary, and anyone will be able to have whatever they want, physical or digital. It's history's cruellest joke: capitalism, not socialism, will deliver the socialist utopia. It won't arrive peacefully.

## Capitalism builds the utopia

Socialism promised a world without want or toil, and every attempt to reach it by planning and redistribution ended in scarcity. The road that actually leads there is the one socialists hated: competition, private capital, and the profit motive driving automation forward until the cost of producing almost anything falls toward zero. Markets build the machines, and the machines end scarcity. Universal high income won't come from a revolution. It will come from a production line that runs itself.

That is the irony. The socialist dream is real, but the only way to it runs straight through capitalism.

## Most work is already pointless

This won't destroy anything most people value. They already don't want the jobs they have. A huge share of modern work is paperwork, compliance, and box-ticking that exists to keep people busy, and much of it could be automated today. The machines won't take away meaningful vocations. They'll take away routines people only tolerate because they need the money.

Look at history honestly and very few people have ever found their meaning in their work. If they had, the world would be full of fulfilled people and humanity would advance faster every year. Work for most people has been a means of survival, nothing more.

## The price of abundance

That's where the real cost lies. Work matters to the human mind, but for most people money is what makes them work at all. Take away the need and most people will do very little. With it goes the structure that work imposes: the hierarchies of skill, status, and achievement that organise human life. A world where everyone can do anything and have everything is a world with no ladder left to climb.

It may also be the end of most human progress. Necessity drove invention, and in a world without necessity, most people stop striving. But there is no version of the utopia that avoids this. Abundance and the loss of hierarchy come together. You can't have one without the other.

## A rough road

The transition will be brutal. Whole classes of work will vanish faster than people can adjust, and the shock will collide with some of the deepest parts of human nature: the need to be useful, to compete, to rank. There will be no smooth, perfectly planned path. Societies will lurch and fight and break things along the way.

But at the end of it lies the end of economics and politics as we know them. Every problem of production and distribution, the questions that have driven every war and revolution, gets solved. Whether that turns out good or bad for the human mind, the material problem itself will be finished.

## Where this leads

When that day comes there will be nothing left to blame. No boss, no system, no scarcity. Just each person facing the question of what to do with a life that no longer has to be earned. Most people lost their sense of meaning long ago, and work only covered for it. Automation will take the cover away.

The only problem left will be you. It always was.
