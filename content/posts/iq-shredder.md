Modernity eats its best people. It finds the brightest, most conscientious minds wherever they are born, pulls them into its cities with work and amusement, and then makes sure they don't reproduce. Intelligence is highly heritable, so every generation that runs through this machine leaves a thinner pool of talent for the next. The machine has a name: the IQ shredder.

## Singapore is the model

Singapore is the purest example. Its founding prime minister used to boast that its selective immigration meant population growth was talent growth: bright Indians, bright Chinese, bright Caucasians. He waved Japan around as the cautionary tale, a nation that refuses migrants and will dissolve into nothing.

But the supply of bright people is not infinite. And what do they do once they arrive? They enter the finance and marketing rat race and push fertility down to 0.78, a level at which each generation is less than half the size of the last. Their genes are spent to keep property prices up and the fine dining full. Japan has 120 million people. How many bright foreigners would it need to import to offset its decline, ten million? Do they even exist in those numbers? And would they feel any loyalty to the Japanese nation if they did?

The mechanics are simple. Build a high-performance capitalist city with order, safety, and opportunity. Make immigration strictly selective. Extract productive work from every available adult. The result is a genetic sink, a black hole for human capital. From a purely economic point of view, talent is wasted anywhere else, so market incentives send it all to the shredder.

## Cities have always been population sinks

This is not new. Urbanisation has always turned children from productive assets into luxury goods. On a farm a child is a pair of hands. In a city a child is a cost centre. School fees alone are the most effective contraceptive ever invented. Even if parents cared about nothing but raising their children well, city costs would shrink family size dramatically.

Then comes the culture. The city takes concrete persons with given roles and turns them into abstract, self-defining individuals who can sign contracts. Feminism, individualism, and secularism all follow from that, and all of them push fertility down. Once women's options extend beyond raising children, families shrink. Large families become a conservative eccentricity, and then a target of political warfare. Subsidise children all you like. The city will still be too expensive and its culture will still take pro-natal habits apart.

Civilisations have run this cycle before. As they rise they pile up knowledge, art, and comfort. The elites who should carry it forward are distracted by it, children become less attractive, and below a critical fertility rate there are too few capable people left to bear the load. The structure becomes top-heavy and falls. It has happened again and again.

## The hinterland runs out

Cities used to refill from their own countryside. In the most advanced countries that countryside has now been consumed. The city keeps replacing its population, but now from abroad. At that point the gap between urban and rural fertility becomes an ethnic gap, and the race question comes back whether anyone wants it or not. That is not intellectuals stirring things up. It is built into the modern process, and no amount of censorship will stop people asking who is actually having children.

## No way back

What would the old right do about it? "Burn the cities" has been floated, and it has a precedent: the Khmer Rouge emptied Phnom Penh in the 1970s. It was a horror then and wouldn't work now. Nobody is going back to the village.

## Outrun it

That leaves the NRx answer, which is to accelerate. If the genetic burn rate is unsustainable, convert human intelligence into self-improving machine capital before the fuel runs out. Hong Kong and Singapore already point the way: resilient ethnic traditions combined with ferocious technical performance, producing open but self-protecting, orderly, high-growth enclaves. The shredder can't be stopped, so the only move left is to automate the minds it consumes.

That should feel horrifying. Bureaucracies are already run by machine-like humans, and the next step is human-like machines absorbing what's left. But nobody reading this on a screen gets to pretend they're outside the system. You're already rootless and half-anonymous, already a product of the shredder. The only choice left is whether to be shredded or to build what comes after.
